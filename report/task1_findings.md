# Task 1 findings (numbers come from outputs/tables/*.csv and eda_findings.json)

## A. Dataset
* Beijing PM2.5 (UCI / Kaggle copies), one monitoring location, 43,824 rows, 1 Jan 2010 00:00 to 31 Dec 2014 23:00.
* Strictly hourly: all 43,823 gaps between consecutive rows are exactly 1 hour, so no timestamps are missing.
* Only `pm2.5` has missing values: 2,067 hours (4.72%). They form 214 stretches (median 1 hour). 158 stretches are 6 hours or shorter (250 hours in total); 56 are longer (1,817 hours), 20 exceed 24 hours, the longest is 155 hours. 2010-2012 hold most gaps (669/728/489 hours) versus 82 and 99 in 2013-2014.
* Handling: for exploratory analysis, linear time interpolation is used ONLY for stretches of 6 hours or fewer. In a hide-and-refill simulation, interpolation MAE is 8.0 (1 h), 13.0 (3 h), 17.7 (6 h), 25.6 (12 h), 36.8 (24 h), and 47.9 (48 h), versus forward fill at 14.2 / 20.3 / 28.3 / 39.3 / 52.3 / 67.0 and global mean at 70.1 / 75.8 / 67.0 / 67.3 / 67.5 / 66.4. Error rises with outage duration. Longer outages stay missing because interpolating across them would impose an increasingly uncertain straight-line trend; 1,817 hours remain missing in the EDA series. For forecasting, PM2.5 input features instead use causal forward fill for at most 6 hours, and future labels are kept only when an actual PM2.5 observation exists. Rows with incomplete feature history or a missing target are excluded. This prevents future readings from leaking into predictors or synthetic targets from entering evaluation.
* PM2.5: mean 98.6, median 72, std 92.1, max 994, skew 1.80. 1,759 hours exceed 300 and 125 exceed 500; the IQR fence is 299 (4.05% flagged). They are real pollution episodes, not sensor errors, so they are kept. The log transform is closer to bell-shaped.
* Other variables: DEWP, TEMP, PRES are roughly symmetric. `Iws` (cumulated wind speed) is strongly right-skewed (skew 4.3), and snow/rain hours are zero-inflated (skew 19.5 / 11.7). Wind direction: SE 34.9%, NW 32.3%, calm/variable (cv) 21.4%, NE 11.4%.

## B. Analytical questions
**Q1 Trend?** No statistically significant trend. Yearly means were 104.1, 99.1, 90.5, 101.7, and 97.6 ug/m3 (2010-2014). A linear fit to daily means has slope -1.18 ug/m3 per year, p = 0.355, and R2 = 0.0005. The 365-day moving average is broadly flat near 100. Hours above 150 per year were 1955, 1809, 1626, 1903, and 1743. The data do not support a sustained improvement over 2010-2014. (Figure Q1)

**Q2 Seasonality?** Yes, but not a clean winter-only bump. Monthly means peak in February (126.1), October (120.3), January (115.2), and November (105.8), and are lowest in August (80.0), May (80.1), and April (83.6). Winter (December-February) averages 112.9 versus 90.3 in summer (June-August), about 25% higher. Figure Q2.

**Q3 Daily cycle?** Winter peaks at 01:00 (143.1) and bottoms at 14:00 (90.8); summer peaks at 07:00 (98.4) and bottoms at 16:00 (80.2). Afternoon is cleanest in both seasons; deeper daytime atmospheric mixing is a plausible explanation, but was not tested here. The winter peak is about 58% above its daily low. Figure Q3.

**Q4 Weather correlation?** Weak linear relationships. Pearson with PM2.5: wind (Iws) -0.25, dew point +0.17, temperature -0.09, pressure -0.05. Spearman (rank) is stronger: wind -0.36, dew point +0.30, pressure -0.14, so the relationships are monotonic but not linear. Median PM2.5 by wind direction: NW 31.0 (cleanest), NE 54.9, SE 91.0, calm/variable 98.0 (dirtiest). DEWP, TEMP and PRES are strongly collinear with each other (0.82, -0.78, -0.83), which matters for linear models. Figure Q4.

**Q5 Lag effects (lagged features)?** Strong persistence. Autocorrelation: 0.965 (1 h), 0.919 (2 h), 0.872 (3 h), 0.745 (6 h), 0.563 (12 h), 0.398 (24 h), 0.153 (48 h), 0.073 (72 h). Memory is roughly 1-2 days, which is why lags of 1, 2, 3, 6, 12 and 24 hours were created. Figure Q5.

**Q6 Moving averages?** Correlation between the PM2.5 value 6 hours later and a trailing moving average of window w: w=1 h 0.745, 3 h 0.720, 6 h 0.689, 12 h 0.644, 24 h 0.585, 48 h 0.455, 72 h 0.376, 168 h 0.233. Longer windows smooth the hourly noise (see the Jan-Feb 2012 zoom) and expose the slow background level, but they react late, so the shortest window is the best single predictor and the correlation falls steadily as the window grows. We keep several windows (3, 6, 12, 24, 72 h) so a model can combine the fresh signal with the background level. Figure Q6.

**Q7 Delayed wind effect (lagged external variable)?** Wind speed correlates most strongly with PM2.5 at lag 0 (-0.248) and the relationship fades smoothly: -0.227 (6 h), -0.198 (12 h), -0.148 (24 h). There is no delayed peak, so the results are consistent with an immediate association plus persistence, not evidence of a delayed causal effect. Mean PM2.5 falls from 137.3 (Iws <= 1) to 106.5 (2-5), 88.6 (10-20), and 41.1 (> 50). Figure Q7.

## C. Models (forecast horizon: 6 hours ahead; train 2010-2012, validation 2013, test 2014)
The chronological split and time-series cross-validation include a six-hour embargo before each boundary, so the future target for a row in one period cannot fall in the next period. Forecast input features use causal forward-filled PM2.5 values (up to 6 hours); target labels use only observed PM2.5, and rows with missing target labels or required feature history are excluded.
See outputs/tables/experiment_table.csv and experiment_table.md. Test RMSE (ug/m3): persistence 61.38; Ridge 55.34; Random Forest without lag/MA features 54.28; Random Forest with lags/MAs 55.09; HistGradientBoosting with lags/MAs 55.38; HistGradientBoosting using PM2.5 history but no weather 57.13.
Selected on 2013 validation RMSE: Random Forest with all features (validation RMSE 61.45). After refitting on training plus validation data, 2014 test metrics are RMSE 53.96, MAE 34.82, and R2 0.671; the persistence baseline has RMSE 61.38 and R2 0.575. The tuned experiments beat persistence by about 7-12%. The four main model test RMSEs are within about 1-2% of one another, so the differences should not be overstated. The history-only vs all-feature HistGradientBoosting results suggest weather contributes useful information (57.13 vs 55.38 RMSE). In this Random Forest comparison, adding lag/moving-average features did not improve test RMSE over the no-lag variant (55.09 vs 54.28). Errors rise with pollution level: MAE is 26.5-28.9 below 150, 45.3 for 150-250, and 90.0 above 250 ug/m3.
